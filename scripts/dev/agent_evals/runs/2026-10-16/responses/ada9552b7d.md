**Verdict:** REJECT

**Steelman the objection.** In the country rows the offsets cannot be identified. Each period has one national number to fit and K cluster offsets, so the fit is under-determined by K-1 (`country_obs_is_per_cluster` in `src/vwf/data.py` says this itself). Started from zero, L-BFGS-B ends close to the minimum-norm solution along the capacity-weighted gradient. The gap gets spread over K clusters, so each offset shrinks roughly as 1/K whatever the bias is. "Within 0.1 m/s" would mostly measure K and the solver's starting point, not whether the offset is needed. The premise is also doubtful. The scalar is obs/sim of capacity factor (correction.py:66), but it multiplies wind speed. Because capacity factor rises steeply with wind speed, the scalar overshoots, and the offset is what pulls that back. A large remaining gap is expected by construction, not a small one.

**Cheaper test.** No optimiser or tolerance change is needed. For each training period, compute the gap obs minus sim(scalar, offset 0) directly. That gap is the quantity in question. The decision that actually matters is cheaper still. Zero the offsets in one country row, score one held-out test year with and without them, then widen to more rows only if the difference is small.

**Simpler explanation.** A pass would follow from three things the proposal does not control:
- the number of clusters (more clusters give smaller offsets each);
- the x0 = 0 start;
- the arbitrary 0.1 m/s cut. Near the steep part of the curve, 0.1 m/s is about 0.005 to 0.01 in capacity factor, which is not negligible.

Refused periods drop out and can push the share up.

**Kill criterion.** None that bears on the claim. The gate concerns how big the offsets are. "Could be dropped" concerns test-year skill, and the proposal reports no test year. A proper kill criterion: test-year error with offsets zeroed is worse than with fitted offsets by more than a stated margin in any row.

**Opportunity cost.** The fit's own comment (harness/corrections.py:433) says the identifiability of country offsets is "an open question studied elsewhere". This proposal answers a weaker form of that question while delaying the study that answers it properly. It also delays the parked #67 weighting preregistration.
