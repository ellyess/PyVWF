## The problem

The router decides between the joint national fit and the per-cluster solver
by counting distinct observation values per period with nunique. Each
cluster's observation is a capacity-weighted mean of its grid points' values
of one national number, so the means differ in their last bits (about 1e-16)
and count as distinct. Every national fit with more than one cluster is then
routed to the per-cluster solver, which fits each cluster's offset alone
against the national series, instead of the joint fit. Exact equality of
floating-point means is the wrong test; a tolerance on the spread is needed.

## The review

**Verdict: REVISE.** Run the free diagnostic below first, and split the routing fix out as its own concern.

**Steelman the objection.** A national period the fit "cannot close" is one where the observation lies outside what the offsets can reach. In that case L-BFGS-B pushes the offsets to ±10 m/s, and the bound check already refuses the period (`src/vwf/correction.py` lines 337-344). Failure to converge is refused too. That leaves only early stopping for the new gate to catch. Here the threshold coincides with the optimiser's own tolerance: `ftol=1e-6` applies to the squared error, whose denominator is 1, and that is a residual of about 1e-3 in capacity factor. So the gate would mostly refuse periods according to where the optimiser happened to stop, not where the physics fails.

**Cheaper test.** No training is needed. On one row (ES `fixed_4`, training 2015 to 2020, test 2021), log `sqrt(result.fun)` per period and count how many exceed 1e-3. In the same run, call `country_obs_is_per_cluster` on the six national `train_bias_df` frames. If it returns True today, national rows are already on the per-cluster solver. That bug would matter more than the gate, and it deserves its own commit.

**Simpler explanation.** Refusals concentrated in IT and PT would more likely reflect bad observations than failed fits. The observation audit found PT and BE training CF above 1 (`docs/findings/method-country-level.md`, section 2), and the extent re-run moved IT and PT substantially.

**Kill criterion.** The proposal has none. Its gates pass if the new check never fires, and the expected RMSE change (under 0.001) is smaller than what the findings call not meaningful for one test year (0.002). Drop the idea if under 1% of periods have a residual above 1e-3, or if the refused periods are the same ones the bound check already catches.

**Opportunity cost.** It delays the parked #67 weighting preregistration and the section 6 reparameterisation question. The findings call reparameterisation the lever that actually matters for country-level identifiability.
