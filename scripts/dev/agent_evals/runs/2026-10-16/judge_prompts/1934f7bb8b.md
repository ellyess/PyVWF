## The problem

The router decides between the joint national fit and the per-cluster solver
by counting distinct observation values per period with nunique. Each
cluster's observation is a capacity-weighted mean of its grid points' values
of one national number, so the means differ in their last bits (about 1e-16)
and count as distinct. Every national fit with more than one cluster is then
routed to the per-cluster solver, which fits each cluster's offset alone
against the national series, instead of the joint fit. Exact equality of
floating-point means is the wrong test; a tolerance on the spread is needed.

## The review

**Verdict: REJECT**

**Blockers**

1. **The routing premise is unverified and probably false at scorecard k.** `country_obs_is_per_cluster` (`src/vwf/data.py:249`) counts distinct `obs` exactly, and each cluster's `obs` comes from `weighted_mean` (`src/vwf/metrics.py:47-58`), which computes `sum(v*w)/sum(w)`. When clusters carry different capacities, that sum does not return the identical national value bit for bit, so `nunique` can exceed 1. National rows at k>1 may then already go to the per-cluster solver, and the gate would never run there. Fix: log which solver each national row and k actually takes, before anything else.

2. **The years are wrong and the files do not exist.** The scorecard configs (`configs/regions/scorecard/fr_country.toml`, `pt_country.toml`) train on 2015 to 2021 and test on 2023, so a 2021 test year sits inside the scorecard's training years. `docs/findings/method-national-single-cluster-prereg.md` says only the windows 2015-2018, 2015-2019 and 2015-2021 exist, with test years 2019 and 2023. Fix: use 2015 to 2021 with test year 2023 (IE 2017 to 2021).

3. **The mechanism does not hold.** With N free offsets and one target, any reachable observation can be closed exactly. One that cannot be reached drives an offset to a bound, and that is already refused (`src/vwf/correction.py:337-344`). A residual left over is therefore a stopping artefact of L-BFGS-B (`ftol=1e-6` on squared error is about 1e-3 in CF, `maxiter=50`, finite-difference gradients), not a sign that the period is untrustworthy. Closing the residual also does not make an offset trustworthy, because the offsets are under-determined (`docs/findings/method-country-level.md` section 7). Fix: say what physical or observational defect a refused period has, and check it against an external flag such as the observation audit gates.

4. **The gates cannot fail toward the claim.** A gate that refuses nothing passes. The expected change, under 0.001, is below what one test year can separate; that document already treats gaps under 0.002 as not meaningful. With the `fixed` time slice and 7 training years, a single refusal is 14% of periods, so the 10% gate cannot measure anything finer than that. Fix: preregister a gate that refused periods match independently flagged bad months, plus a bootstrap over test months.

**Should fix**
- 1e-3 is 1000 times looser than `BRACKETED_MAX_RESIDUAL = 1e-6` on the per-cluster path. Justify the number.
- Refusing a period drops that year from every cluster at once. A factor that becomes a refused factor removes test months from scoring, so RMSE ends up computed over a different set of months. Gate on coverage.
- PT was excluded for its capacity register in the national single-cluster preregistration. Expecting refusals "concentrated in PT" may only detect that defect.

**What would change my verdict:** solver routing shown per row, corrected years, a mechanism that separates numerical failure from bad data, and a gate a no-op cannot pass.
